Battery-Powered Wireless Mice

Logitech

  • G304 (2.4GHz) 1×AA — This mouse can be modded into almost anything you want: turned into a GPW clone, fitted with wireless charging, or retrofitted with a built-in lithium battery — quite extreme. [Lightweight small mouse, turbo lithium battery for perfect center of gravity] Uses Logitech Gaming Software; will disconnect when battery is low but doesn't affect the experience — just swap the battery.
  • G603 (2.4GHz/Bluetooth dual-mode) 2×AA — [Heavy large mouse, perfect center of gravity] Excellent battery life; not recommended for gaming due to weight. Can run on one battery but causes significant center-of-gravity shift.
  • G604 (2.4G/Bluetooth dual-mode) 1×AA — [Heavy large mouse]
  • G309 (2.4GHz) 1×AA

Razer

  • Basilisk X Hyperspeed (commonly "Little Basilisk"; 2.4GHz/Bluetooth dual-mode) 1×AA — [Light medium-large mouse, perfect center of gravity] Good battery life, strong performance, but very poor quality control — prone to deep sleep issues. Doesn't affect normal use; buyers beware.
  • Basilisk V3X Hyperspeed (commonly "Little Basilisk 2"; 2.4GHz/Bluetooth dual-mode) 1×AA
  • Razer Atheris (2.4GHz/Bluetooth dual-mode) — [Lightweight portable mouse]
  • Razer Orochi V2 (2.4GHz/Bluetooth) — [Lightweight portable mouse]
  • Razer DeathAdder V2 X Hyperspeed (2.4GHz/Bluetooth) 1×AA/1×AAA — [Light large mouse, perfect center of gravity] Specs: PAW3319 (14000 DPI/300 IPS/35G), dual battery compartment (AA/AAA), 235 hours battery life (2.4GHz), 615 hours (Bluetooth), official weight 86-103g (depending on battery). Includes 1×AA battery. Perfect battery compartment layout, perfect center of gravity. Razer 2nd-gen green 70M switches, 70 million click lifespan. 1 set of onboard memory. 125Hz polling rate in Bluetooth mode. In 2.4G mode, no noticeable latency. In Bluetooth mode on Windows there's some stuttering, but connecting to iPad Pro is much better — some latency but far less than on Windows. In terms of Bluetooth experience, I'd say it soundly beats the Little Basilisk, though the Little Basilisk can improve polling rate via firmware updates — whether that closes the gap in smoothness, I'm not sure. Razer's hereditary issue is failing to wake from hibernation — diplomatically put, "extreme power saving" (though still can't beat Logitech). In practice, can't use both batteries simultaneously. In terms of center of gravity, it's flawless — the most perfect balance I've used. For a large mouse, this weight performance is excellent, but the shell feels a bit thin. Top cover design references the G603 — magnetic top cover, easy battery replacement, but requires technique during installation. Design has some issues. The sensor's paper specs are modest, but positioning accuracy is quite good — personally I think slightly better than the G304's HERO 12K. In terms of stability and battery life, HERO wins. Of course, compared to some flagships, I think there's still a gap. The mouse has no side skirt design, which I really appreciate — as a sweaty-handed user, I reject any mouse with side skirts. I got it for 224 yuan, though probably not the historical low.
  • Viper V3 Hyperspeed 1×AA

ASUS

  • ROG Gladius III (2.4/Bluetooth) — [Lightweight portable mouse]
  • ROG Keris III (2.4/Bluetooth) 1×AA
  • ASUS TUF Gaming Wireless Dual-Mode Mouse (2.4GHz/Bluetooth) — [Lightweight "unknown" mouse] 232 hours battery life (Bluetooth mode), 62g weight (unloaded), PBT keycaps, includes 1×AA and 1×AAA battery plus battery converter. Sensor model unknown (12000 DPI/35G acceleration/350 IPS max speed). Paper performance is slightly worse than products with PAW3335.

SteelSeries

  • Rival 3 Wireless (2.4GHz/Bluetooth) 2×AAA — [Slightly heavy medium mouse, rear-weighted center of gravity] High power consumption mouse — the world's first mass-produced RGB battery-powered mouse. Two AAA batteries. For me, a very comfortable mouse; claw grip feel ranks first. But when you pick it up, it's surprising because the front-to-back height difference is quite large. This can be improved by using turbo or other mouse-specific batteries (similar issue to the G304, but more severe).

Daryou

  • Daryou A918 Wireless Dual-Mode Mouse (2.4GHz/Bluetooth) 1×AA — [Medium-small mouse, right-handed ergonomic gaming mouse] PAW3335 (16000 DPI/400 IPS/40G), right-handed ergonomic mouse, AA battery compartment, includes 1×AA battery. Battery life: 260 hours (1000Hz) or 6 months (125Hz), though it's not specified whether this is for 2.4GHz or Bluetooth. 30 million click Daryou custom microswitches (model unknown), 85g weight (unloaded).
