Monitor Buying Guide

Buying Guide Monitors Updated 2022-10-23 · Sect Master

藏
As one of the core components of a computer, the monitor has the greatest impact on user experience. Many monitors appear to be best-sellers with seemingly good specs, but have poor build quality and aren't worth buying. Many monitors are also harsh on the eyes and uncomfortable to look at.

1. Size

The most popular monitor sizes currently sold online include 21.5", 23.8"/24", 24.8"/25", 27", 32", ultrawide, etc.

At the same viewing distance, a larger screen size requires a greater neck rotation angle.

A large portion of users sit about 40cm from their monitors. At this distance, a 24" screen only requires eye movement, while a 27" screen requires both neck and eye movement. So at this distance, 23.8", 24", or 25" monitors are the best choices.

The Sect Master sits about 80cm to 100cm from the monitor. At this distance, a 27" feels the same as a 24" at 40cm — the optimal experience.

2. Resolution

Higher resolution monitors produce finer images without losing too much detail — this is why bigger isn't always better.

Currently mainstream resolutions:

  • 1080P/FHD: 1920×1080
  • 2K/QHD: 2560×1440
  • 4K/UHD: 3840×2160
  • 8K/UHD: 7680×4320

Based on a 40cm viewing distance, good size and resolution pairings are: 24"/1080p, 27"/2K, 32"/4K.

Based on the Sect Master's 80cm viewing distance, good pairings are: 24"/900p, 27"/1080p, 32"/2K.

3. Refresh Rate

Refresh rate refers to the number of times the screen image is redrawn per second — how many times the screen is refreshed and how many frames are displayed within one second. A higher refresh rate means better image stability and smoother transitions.

The human eye perceives the most noticeable difference from 30Hz to 60Hz, then from 60Hz to 75Hz it's fairly noticeable, from 75Hz to 120Hz less noticeable, and above 120Hz the human eye can barely tell the difference.

So for non-hardcore gamers, choosing within the 60Hz to 120Hz range is recommended. Casual gamers can go with 75Hz, and office users who don't play games can choose 60Hz.

4. Response Time

To get straight to the point: office users should choose monitors with GTG (gray-to-gray) response time of 2ms-10ms, while FPS gamers should choose monitors with GTG of 1ms-2ms. These are typically Nano/Fast IPS/TN panels.

5. Color Gamut

Primarily refers to the monitor's color reproduction range (also applies to photos). The full coverage area is the visible spectrum of the human eye. Some people may have difficulty distinguishing red from green (color blindness). Similar to hearing in music, professionals have higher color discrimination than the average person.
  • sRGB: Co-created by Microsoft and HP, commonly used in Windows operating systems and software
  • NTSC: Color standard for electronic TV screens; covering 100% sRGB ≈ 72% NTSC
  • Adobe RGB: Covers sRGB + CMYK color space, primarily a standard for graphic design
  • DCI-P3: A newer color standard for digital cinema, focused on the human visual experience

Apple computers use the DCI-P3 wide gamut at 93% coverage.

Art students are recommended to choose monitors matching Apple's monitor color gamut standards.

6. Color Depth

Why do Apple iPads and computers look so comfortable? Besides high resolution, most Apple devices have higher color depth for smoother gradients — 8-bit = 16.7 million colors, 10-bit = 1.07 billion colors. Good color depth provides very smooth visual transitions with no visible banding.

VA panels can typically only reach 8-bit, while IPS panels can reach 10-bit. Some monitor manufacturers provide larger processing chips, such as 14-bit color mixing precision, to improve color accuracy and provide better color transitions.

Most monitors available online are native 8-bit. Those labeled as 10-bit are generally 8-bit dithered to 10-bit — i.e., fake 10-bit. True native 10-bit monitors are relatively expensive.

7. Color Accuracy

Why do actual colors differ so much from what's shown on the monitor? The color accuracy issue that advertising and fashion designers care most about will greatly affect the final color rendering delivered to clients.

Delta E represents color accuracy — the lower the value, the more accurate the color. To meet the stringent requirements of professional photographers and image workers, monitors should generally have Delta E ≤ 2 (average). If Delta E is too high, colors will be distorted and require professional calibration. A monitor isn't better just because colors are more saturated — what matters is how close it is to reality. Generally, Delta E below 3 is normal, and good ones are below 1.5.

8. Monitor Interfaces

Monitor interfaces connect to the computer's graphics card via data cables for data transmission. Higher-end monitors have stricter interface requirements — some 144Hz 2K monitors can't perform properly, possibly because a DP cable isn't being used.
  • HDMI: The latest specification is HDMI 2.1, which supports 4K 120Hz and 8K 60Hz, supports High Dynamic Range (HDR), can optimize per scene or frame, and is backward compatible with HDMI 2.0 and HDMI 1.4
  • DP: Mainly includes DisplayPort and Apple's miniDP interface, with ultra-high transmission bandwidth, supporting ultra-high resolution and 30-bit color depth. DP 1.4 supports 4K at 60-240Hz, and is currently the most widely supported interface by graphics cards
  • Type-C/Thunderbolt: Type-C is similar to DP 1.2 specs, but Apple's enhanced Thunderbolt interface can support 5K video and up to 8-channel audio simultaneously
  • VGA: The oldest interface, mainly 1080P resolution, theoretically up to 2048×1536, susceptible to interference
  • DVI: Resolution up to 2K+, but refresh rate is only 60Hz, and color depth support is limited to 8-bit

9. Panels

Display panel comparison:

  • Color and accuracy: IPS > VA > TN
  • Response time: TN > IPS > VA
  • Viewing angle: IPS > VA > TN
  • Contrast: VA > IPS > TN

Panel material comparison:

  • TN panel: Fast response, good for competitive gaming, but inaccurate colors
  • VA panel: High contrast, purer blacks (current models have improved in quality)
  • IPS panel: Wide viewing angles, strong color accuracy, the top choice for image work, but backlight bleeding is common

10. Sync Technology: G-Sync, Free-Sync

What are G-Sync and Free-Sync for? In gaming, if the graphics card output isn't synchronized, the image will experience tearing and double images. Dynamic refresh rate sync technology was added to effectively prevent screen tearing, which is especially important for high-resolution, high-quality visuals.

Currently there are mainly 2 types of monitor sync technology plus one FreeSync variant: Adaptive-Sync

  • FreeSync (AMD monitor sync technology)
  • G-SYNC (NVIDIA monitor sync technology)
  • Adaptive-Sync (universal FreeSync/G-SYNC)
  • Through the DP interface, NVIDIA cards can also support FreeSync monitors
